  §3.4  D24.4 (π_R1 and the opened part of C1Lift) in the same comparison.
  D24.4 says that under π_R1 the eligible semantic coefficients of each
  column form the top block of quarter indices i ∈ [208, 256) (classes
  s = 0, 1 exactly that block of 48; classes 2, 3 a subset of it plus sparse
  i ≡ 3 mod 4), so the R0 Vandermonde argument (opened_prefix) does not
  transfer, and names Variant D (D13 transport) or a digit-map search (J11b).
  Observation (STRUCTURAL by (E1); not in the (ii′) design, to be checked by
  the lead against the registry): the top block contains the dyadically
  aligned sub-block i ∈ [224, 246).  Since 224 = 2^5 + 2^6 + 2^7 and every
  e < 32 has bits only in 0..4, N_{224+e} = N_224·N_e for e < 22, so on that
  sub-block the 22-node system is diag(N_224(X_u))·Vandermonde(N_0..N_21) —
  invertible for EVERY set of 22 distinct nodes once N_224(X_u) =
  T_32·T_64·T_128(X_u) ≠ 0, which is the (NZ) circle-order fact already
  recorded as a J7a/J10 obligation (X_u is the x-coordinate of a point of
  order 2^20, and T_{2^k}(x(P)) = x(2^k P) = 0 only for 2^k P of order 4).
  The (ii′) design's "N_192·g, g ∈ span{N_16..N_63}, non-Vandermonde"
  analysis uses the whole block; a 22-element aligned sub-block suffices for
  surjectivity onto the 88 opened values.  Hence:
   - classes s = 0, 1: the opened part of C1Lift is STRUCTURAL on R1 as built
     (no Variant D, no digit-map change), given NZ for k = 5, 6, 7;
   - classes s = 2, 3: structural iff their eligible subset contains some
     aligned block c + [0, 22) with c ≡ 0 (mod 32), c + 21 < 256, inside the
     eligible rows — a finite check of the registry (minutes, no search over
     4!⁵·120 maps); if not, the class needs a different aligned block
     (c ≡ 0 mod 2^m for a block of size 2^m ≥ 22 fits only m = 5) or the
     remaining-limbs premise C1T-open absorbs it.
  Cost comparison if the observation fails for classes 2, 3:
   - Variant D (D13 transport with two folds): restores opened_prefix
     verbatim, but reintroduces the transport component of the verifier's
     weight evaluation that D18 removed by construction (recorded as the
     "3.5M transport component of R-E4's V2" in D18.1; even a 10× better
     implementation is far beyond either transaction's margin). Not viable on
     R1's budget.
   - J11b digit-map search: product-form digit permutations keep D18.1's O(1)
     dual folds, so verifier cost ≈ 0 if a map exists; cost is a probe and a
     re-run of the Z9/Z5 gates.
   - M3 (§3.3) also covers it: C1T-open and the opened part of C1Lift are
     rank conditions on public data, so they join P and are enforced by
     rejection at zero verifier cost.
  None of M1/M2 helps here (they act on the mask lanes, not on the semantic
  lanes' eligible noise).

§4  Recommendation for D23.5 (and D24.4)
---------------------------------------
The binding constraint is the R1 compute budget, not the mathematics.  Both
Route M changes that remove a premise inside the verifier cost tens of
thousands of CU in transactions that are already within 31.5k (stage2, worst
settlement) and 57k (stage1, which is 43k over the 1.3M target) of the cap.

Recommendation.
  1. D23.5: adopt M3 (honest-prover rejection on the public predicate P),
     and keep M1 as an optional hardening if ~25–70k CU of headroom appears.
     - M3 is the only option that removes the transversality family from the
       privacy statement without touching the verifier, the proof bytes or
       any soundness row.  Every proved theorem (T2a, T2b-1, T2b-2a, ZF-A, J1,
       J3, J5a) and the planned T2b-2b statement (premises as hypotheses of
       P) stay as they are.  The price is paid where work is open anyway: ZF1
       must be stated for a prover with aborts; the honest prover runs the
       exact P check (seconds per attempt); and the Rust check must be shown
       to decide the Lean P on the R1 payload of Z9.  The same mechanism also
       drops Good₃'s mass and C1T-open/H1T-open from the privacy error.
     - M1 is cheap in Lean and proves B-open away in a few lines (on R1 it
       replaces "G alone covers B₀" by "Z alone covers B₀, structurally"); it is
       the standard ZK-FRI mask.  It does nothing for OT-open, and its
       +24–69k CU does not fit stage2's worst margin today.
     - M2 removes OT-open and Good₃'s round-cover condition but costs
       ≈ +55k CU in stage2 (31.5k left) and still leaves one numerical
       certificate.
     - Route P should not be the main line: the available tools have a hard
       capacity limit (Kronecker ≤ 5 functionals against ~400–1,900 in the
       premises; the R1 form V_G = B₀ is the largest), and the dimension
       heuristic that explains the numerics is not a proof.  Z10 (§A) finds no
       failure on random or structured query sets of an R1-sized model, which
       supports M3's completeness assumption (Pr[¬P] ≈ 0) but bounds nothing.
  2. D24.4: before choosing Variant D or J11b, check the aligned sub-block of
     §3.4.  If classes 2 and 3 also contain an aligned 22-block of eligible
     coefficients, the opened part of C1Lift is structural on R1 as built at
     zero cost; if not, J11b (≈ 0 CU) or M3 cover it.  Variant D is not
     affordable on R1.
Trade-off stated plainly.  M3 gives a privacy statement free of the
transversality premises at zero on-chain cost, in exchange for a prover that
may retry, an abort-aware Fiat–Shamir theorem, and the obligation that the
deployed prover really runs the P check.  M1+M2 give a premise-free statement
(up to one certified determinant) for any honest prover with no aborts, at
roughly 65–145k CU and +3 KB that R1 does not currently have.  Proving the
premises as they stand is unlikely to succeed.
