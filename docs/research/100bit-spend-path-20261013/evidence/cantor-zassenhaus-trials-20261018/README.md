# Random linear splitting and bounded retries

Four focused Lean results close a concrete randomized splitting subproblem. They are not a complete list decoder or efficient witness extractor.

- `CantorZassenhausPair` proves that two independent uniform field elements define a random linear polynomial with independent uniform evaluations at any two distinct points. For an odd finite field, the probability those evaluations have the same quadratic-character category (zero, square, nonsquare) is at most one half. The exact formula is `2 * sameCategoryCount = (q-1)^2 + 2`.
- `CantorZassenhausSplit` proves different categories at two actual roots produce a proper gcd split. Every gcd is normalized first. It proves normalization preserves roots and divisibility, gives a monic gcd for nonzero input, and proves the factor-quality tests imply positive degree strictly below the parent degree when both polynomials are monic.
- `CantorZassenhausFastTrial` connects this to binary modular exponentiation and an explicit input q with proof q = card K. The success test decides only equality of normalized gcds with 1 and the parent; gcd divisibility is proved, not assumed as a free proposition. It proves the actual two-gcd trial fails with probability at most one half over two uniform field elements. The trial checks gcd(f,h) before gcd(f,h^(q/2)-1); omitting the zero-category check does not justify the exact one-half bound.
- `CantorZassenhausRetries` proves the finite independent-tape product identity and failure at most 2^-t. Its numeric theorem is CONDITIONAL: at most 395,412,192 split sites, with 149 independent trials per site, give a split-event union budget strictly below 2^-120.

The site count is not yet proved for an implemented extractor. Fresh independent sampling at adaptive split nodes, conversion from the actual field representation to the uniform finite-field law, the complete factor recursion, RR tree/caller counts and bit/field-operation costs remain open. Any other sampler/timeout failures must share the extractor failure budget; the 149-trial inequality does not cover them. No practical running-time benchmark is asserted. These are mathematical polynomial models; they are not a complete executable extraction backend.

The risk-first review caught two details before an end-to-end claim: zero-category splitting is necessary for the exact probability bound, and Euclidean gcd is not automatically monic. Earlier raw-gcd focused versions remain in history; their conditional algebraic statements were valid, but raw tests must not be interpreted as a correct proper-degree factor algorithm. Accepted sources use normalized gcds throughout. The previously committed complete-root certificate also used raw gcd equality; its conditional theorem remains valid, but normalization is needed for a general certificate-availability theorem. That supplement is the next target.

The accepted compiles use native pinned Lean 4.32, one bounded local job at a time, aggregate Lean RSS limit 4 GiB and process memory limit 2048 MiB. Exact source revision, source/object/import hashes, wall time, RSS and process swaps are in each receipt. All accepted targets exited zero and every printed axiom report contains only standard axioms. No full replay was run. Existing host swap is recorded separately and is not claimed zero. No production source or protocol parameter changed.
